Most of variables in physics are continuos. But, when we try to put them in the digital world we made them discrete, meaning they have a finite number.
Charles Babbage built an early digital system using variables with ten discrete values.
Nowadays, most computers use a binary representation instead of 10. Because is easier to say that a current is 1 and no current is 0, instead of 10 levels of current.
>[!note] Amount of information (D)
>The amount of information in a discrete valued variable with $N$ distinct states is measured in units of bits as 
>$D = \log_{2}N$ bits (1.1)

A binary variable conveys $\log_{2}2 = 1$ bit of information. Indeed, the word bit is short for *binary digit*. 
A continuous signal theoretically contains an infinite amount of information because it can take on an infinite number of values. In practice, noise and measurement error limit the information to only 10 to 16 bits for most continuous signals. 
*If the measurement must be made rapidly, the information content is lower*. (e.g 8 bits).
We use boolean logic to approach operating on binary variables.
The beauty of this, is that we don't need to worry on details of the work of hardware. But, focusing on make bigger abstract problems.
>[!warning] ¿It's important know the hardware?
>YES YES YES. Understanding the details of the hardware allows the programmer to optimize the software better for that specific computer.
