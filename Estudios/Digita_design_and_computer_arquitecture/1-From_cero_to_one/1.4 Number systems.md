# 1.4.2 Binary numbers
The bit is just one of two values, 0 or 1.
Each column of a binary number has twice the weight of the previous column, so binary numbers are base $2$.
A $N$-bit binary number represents one of $2^{N}$ possibilities: $0,1,2,3,\dots,2^{N}-1$. 
**Conversion: Binary to decimal conversion**.

# 1.4.4 Bytes, Nibbles, and All that Jazz
>[!note] Byte
>A group of eight bits

It represents one of $2^{8} = 256$ possibilities. The computers are measured in bytes rather than bits.
>[!note] Nibble
>A group of four bits, or half a byte 

Represents $2^{4} = 16$ possibilities. 
One hexadecimal digit stores one nibble and two hexadecimal digits store one full byte.
Microprocessors handle data in chunks called *words*. The size of a word depends on the architecture of the microprocessor.
Within a group of bits, the bit in the 1's column is calles **The least significant bit(lsb)**, and the bit at the other end is called the **most significant bit (msb)**
Similarly, within a word, the bytes are identifies as **least significant byte(LSB)** through **most significant byte(MSB)**.
![[Range_of_the_system.png]]
1025 bytes is called a *kilobyte(KB)* or kibibyte (KiB). 1024 bits is called a kilobit (Kb or Kbit) or kibibit. And the others are similar.

# 1.4.6 Signed binary numbers
How could we represents negative numbers?
We have two options *sign/magnitude* and *two's complement*.
## Sign/Magnitude Numbers
An $N-bit$ sign/magnitude number uses the most significant bit as the sign and the remaining $N-1$ bits as the magnitude. A sign bit of 0 indicates positiva and a sign bit of 1 indicates negative.
<span style="color:	#87CEEB">Example:</span> -5 -> $1101_{2}$ and 5 would be $0101_{2}$ Because five is $101_{2}$.
But arithmetically this does not support.
An $N$-bit sign/magnitude number spans the range $[-2^{N-1} + 1, 2^{N-1}-1]$.
Remember, it's gonna be half of the range.
However, sign/magnitude numbers are slightly odd in that both +0 and -0 exists. It can be troublesome to have different representation of the same number.
## Two's Complement Numbers
Two's complement numbers are identical to unsigned binary numbers except that the most significant bit position has a weight of $-2^{N-1}$ instead of $2^{N-1}$. 0 is one time represented and arithmetic's work.
Zero is represented as always.
The most positive number has a 0 in the most significant position and 1's elsewhere: $01\dots 111_{2} = 2^{N-1}-1$. The most negative number has a 1 in the most significant position and 0's elsewhere: $10\dots 000_{2} = -2^{N-1}$. And -1 is written as all ones: 11...111.
The sign of a two's complement number is reversed. By inverting the bits in the number and then adding 1 to the least significant bit position. 
**Reversing the sign** method. 
WHY WE ADD 1. Because if not we'll had two representation of ceros. SEE THIS:
El complemento a uno tiene **dos representaciones para el cero**: `0000` (+0) y `1111` (−0).
<span style="color:	#87CEEB">example:</span> $-2_{10}$ as a 4-bit two's complement number.
First represent the magnitude: $+2_{10} = 0010_{2}$. To get $-2_{10}$, reverse the sign by inverting the bits and adding 1. Inverting 0010 produces $1101_{2}$. $1101_{2}+1 = 1110_{2}$. so, $-2_{10}$ is $1110_{2}$.
Two's complement numbers have the compelling advantage that addition works properly for both positive and negative numbers. Recall that when adding $N$-bit numbers, the carry out of the $N$th bit (i.e, the $N+1^{th}$ result bit) is discarded.
In the book there are plenty examples of sums and things like that.
Like unsigned numbers, N-bit two's complement numbers represent one of $2^{N}$ possible values. But the numbers are split in between positive and negative numbers.
The range of an $N$-bit two's complement spans $[-2^{N-1},2^{N-1}-1]$
Unlike unsigned numbers, a carry out of the most significant column does not indicate overflow. Instead, overflow occurs if the two numbers being added have the same sign bit and the result has the opposite sign bit.
This is overflow. $4_{10} + 5_{10} = 0100_{2}+ 0101_{2} = 1001_{2}= -7_{10}$ .
