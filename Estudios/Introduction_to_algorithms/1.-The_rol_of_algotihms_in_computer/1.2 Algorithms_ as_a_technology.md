## Efficiency
There are some algorithms that depending on how it is solved, the speed would be faster, and differences are greater than hardware and software.
For example, **insertion sort** takes time roughly equal to $c_{1}n^{2}$ to sort $n$ items, where $c_{1}$ is a constant. But, **merge sort**, takes time roughly equal to $c_{2}n\log n$. Insertion sort has typically a smaller constant factor than merge sort, however as $n$ increases, the constant does not affect as $n$ does. There will always be a crossover point beyond which merge sort is faster.
There is a well illustrated example in the book [[Introduction_to_algorithms-3rd Edition.pdf]] p.12
In few words, many times better algorithms can win over a raw power of a excellent computer with a bad algorithm. ^547862
