>[!note] Schematic
>Is a diagram of a digital circuit showing the elements and the wires that connect them together.

<span style="color:	#87CEEB">Example of an schematic</span> 
$$
Y = \bar{A} \bar{B} \bar{c} + A \bar{B} \bar{C} + A \bar{B} C
$$
![[schematic_image.png]]
There are general guidelines to make a schematic. We incentive you to look at the book. (p.67)
Remember this:
- Wires connect at a T juntion
- Wires connect at a dot
- Wires crossing without a dot do not connect.
We can convert to a better schematic, following certain rules. For example, the AND with inverted inputs is equivalent to a NOR gate.
When we have multiple outputs, we should make a truth table for all of them, but, if the logic is clear, you won't need to do that. 
Also, there is X if don't care. For example in a **priority circuit** if a >b b in priority, does not care what value b could get, what matters is if a is 1. If so, then the output would be 1. In that way we can reduce computing every possible output, and put just don't cares. (example in the book p.69).
