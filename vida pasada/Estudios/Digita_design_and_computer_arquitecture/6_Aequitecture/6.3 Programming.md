### Shift instructions.
Shift instrucitons shift the value in a register left or right, dropping bits off the end. 
RISC-V shift operations are sll (shift left logical), srl (shift right logical), and sra (shift right arithmetic).
1. **SLL (Shift Left Logical)**
- **Operation**: Shifts the bits to the **left** and fills the **right** with **zeros**.
- **Example**:
    - **Original**: `0001 1010` (decimal 26)
    - **Shift left by 2**:
      ```
        0001 1010 → (sll by 2) → 0101 1000 (decimal 104)
        ```
- **Explanation**: The leftmost bits are discarded, and the rightmost bits are filled with zeros.
2. **SRL (Shift Right Logical)**
- **Operation**: Shifts the bits to the **right** and fills the **left** with **zeros**.
- **Example**:
    - **Original**: `0001 1010` (decimal 26)
    - **Shift right by 2**:
        ```
        0001 1010 → (srl by 2) → 0000 0110 (decimal 6)
        ```
- **Explanation**: The rightmost bits are discarded, and the leftmost bits are filled with zeros.
3. **SRA (Shift Right Arithmetic)*
- **Operation**: Shifts the bits to the **right** and fills the **left** with the **sign bit** (the leftmost bit).
- **Example** (for a **negative number**):
    - **Original**: `1110 1010` (decimal `-22` in two's complement)
    - **Shift right by 2**:
        ```
        1110 1010 → (sra by 2) → 1111 1010 (decimal -6)
        ```
- **Explanation**: The rightmost bits are discarded, and the leftmost bits are filled with **1's** (the sign bit), preserving the negative value.
4 **Immediate Shift Versions (SLLI, SRLI, SRAI)**
- **Operation**: Similar to the regular shift instructions, but the shift amount is provided as an **immediate** value (a constant, not a register).
    - **SLLI**: Shift left logical by an immediate amount.    
    - **SRLI**: Shit right logical by an immediate amount.
    - **SRAI**: Shift right arithmetic by an immediate amount.
- **Example**:
    - **Original**: `0001 1010` (decimal 26)
    - **Shift left by 2 (SLLI)**:
        ```
        0001 1010 → (slli by 2) → 0101 1000 (decimal 104)
        ```

Shifting a value left by N is equivalent to multiplying it by $2^{N}$. Likewise, shifting a value right by N is equivalent to divifing it by $2^{N}$.Arithmetic right shifts divide two’s complement numbers, while logical right shifts divide unsigned numbers.
Logical shifts are also used with *and* and *or* instructions to exract or assemble bitfields. For example, the following code extracts bits 15:8 from s7 and places them in the lower byte of s6. If s7 is 0x1234ABCD, the s6 will be 0xAB after this code completes.

### Multiply Instructions*
To multiply we use an extension of RISC-V..
The multiply instruction (mul) multiplies two 32-bit numbers and produces a 32-bit product. *mul s1,s2,s3* multiplies the values in *s2* and *s3* and places the least significant 32 bits of the product in s1; the most significant 32 bits of the product are discarded.
Three versions of the “multiply high” operation exist: mulh, mulhsu, and mulhu. These instructions put the high 32 bits of the multiplication result in the destination register.
mulh (multiply high signed signed) treats both operands as signed. mulhsu (multiply high signed unsigned) treats the first operand as signed and the second as unsigned, and mulhu (multiply high unsigned unsigned) treats both operands as unsigned.

### Additional Arguments and Local Variables
Functionas, as we said earlier may have more than eight input arguments and may have to many local variables to keep in preserved registers. Stack is suitable to store this information.
![[Screenshot from 2025-10-15 11-57-16.png]]

Additional arguments are passed on the the stack, just above sp. The caller must expand its stack to make room for the additional arguments.
the **callee does not have to save the arguments** just because they’re arguments. The figure/text is saying:

- **a0–a7 and t0–t6 are _caller-saved_**.  
    If your function (the callee) will **call another function**, that child call is allowed to overwrite `a0–a7` and `t*`. So **if you still need those values after you make a subcall**, you must first **spill (save) them somewhere**—either into your own stack frame or into `s*` registers you’ve saved. That’s what the box “a0–a7, t0–t6 (if needed)” means: _spill slots_, not “store arguments by default”.
    
- **s0–s11 are _callee-saved_**.  
    If your function wants to use any `s*`, you must save/restore them (that’s why the “s0–s11 (if needed)” area exists).
    
- **`ra`**: if the callee will call another function using `jal`, it must **save `ra`** first (otherwise the new `jal` would overwrite it). Hence “`ra (if needed)`”.
    
- **More than 8 arguments**: the _caller_ puts extra args on the stack **in the caller’s frame** (the “argument build area”). When the callee starts, `sp` is the same as the caller’s `sp`, so the callee can **read** those extra args from memory _above_ `sp`. This is the one allowed case where the callee accesses memory outside its own frame. The callee still doesn’t have to _save_ them—only if it needs to keep them across a subcall.
