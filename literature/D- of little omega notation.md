<span style="color:yellow">IDEA:</span>
$$
\omega (g(n)) = \{ f(n): \text{ for any positive constant c > 0, there exists a constant } n_{0} > 0 \text{ such that } 0 \leq cg(n) <f(n) \text{ for all } n \geq n_{0}\}
$$
Another way of defining is:
$$
f(n) = \omega(g(n)) \iff g(n) = o(f(n))
$$
By limits we'll have
$$
\lim_{ n \to \infty } \frac{f(n)}{g(n)} = \infty
$$



<span style="color:yellow">Evidencia:</span>
It is meaning as a lower bound that is not asymptotically tight.
If the limit exist it means that $g(n)$ becomes relatively small compared to to $f(n)$ as $n$ approaches infinitive.
